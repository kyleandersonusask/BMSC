# CLAIMS: blue-white-screening

| # | Claim | Status |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | E. coli beta-galactosidase: 1,024 aa per subunit (about 116 kDa); active only as a tetramer. | TEXTBOOK |
| C2 | lacZdeltaM15 (the omega fragment, carried by the host, often on an F') lacks residues 11-41 and cannot form the active tetramer on its own. | TEXTBOOK |
| C3 | The plasmid's lacZ-alpha encodes a short N-terminal peptide that includes residues 11-41. Added to omega, it restores the tetramer and the activity. Its length depends on the vector (pUC: roughly a hundred codons including the MCS). | TEXTBOOK; length drawn schematically |
| C4 | The MCS sits in frame inside lacZ-alpha. An insert there breaks the frame or adds stop codons, so no working alpha is made and the colony stays white. | TEXTBOOK |
| C5 | Caveat, not drawn: a short in-frame insert can leave a light-blue colony. | TEXTBOOK (omitted for clarity) |
| C6 | Beta-galactosidase cuts X-gal into galactose and 5-bromo-4-chloro-3-hydroxyindole. Two of these dimerise and oxidise to an insoluble blue indigo dye (5,5'-dibromo-4,4'-dichloro-indigo). | TEXTBOOK |
| C7 | Why alpha is small: it only has to supply what omega lacks; the host carries the rest (about 970 aa). The vector avoids carrying all 3 kb of lacZ, which keeps it small (high copy number, good transformation, room for inserts). | TEXTBOOK; REASONED |
| C8 | Ampicillin selects for cells with the plasmid; IPTG binds LacI and frees the lac promoter. | TEXTBOOK |
